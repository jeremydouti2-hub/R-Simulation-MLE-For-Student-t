\section*{Application to Real Data}

In this section, a performance comparison between the proposed estimates
is carried out under the SRS and RSS schemes using the NHANES body
measurements dataset. The dataset contains anthropometric measurements of
$n = 507$ participants. Two variables were selected based on
Kolmogorov--Smirnov (K-S) goodness-of-fit screening: Knee Girth
(\texttt{kne\_gi}) and Calf Girth (\texttt{cal\_gi}), which achieved
K-S p-values of $0.7505$ and $0.6621$, respectively, confirming excellent
fit to the two-parameter Student's $t$-distribution with fixed degrees of
freedom $\nu = 10$.

For Knee Girth, the MLEs of the location parameter $\mu$ and scale
parameter $\sigma$ of the $t(\nu, \mu, \sigma)$ distribution, estimated
via the EM algorithm over the full population ($n = 507$), are
$\hat{\mu} = 36.1400$ and $\hat{\sigma} = 2.3591$, with standard errors
$\mathrm{SE}(\hat{\mu}) = 0.1195$ and $\mathrm{SE}(\hat{\sigma}) = 0.0886$,
respectively. The K-S statistic is $0.0300$, and its associated p-value is
$0.7505$. Figure~\ref{fig:figure2} corroborates these findings by displaying
the empirical CDF versus the estimated CDF $F(x;\hat{\mu},\hat{\sigma})$,
the estimated PDF $f(x;\hat{\mu},\hat{\sigma})$ overlaid on the histogram,
and the probability--probability (PP) plot of the estimated model.

For the analysis, random samples of sizes $n = 5$, $n = 6$, and $n = 7$
were observed under the SRS and RSS schemes with different set sizes and
number of cycles. The MLEs, their standard errors, 95\% confidence
intervals, K-S statistic, and K-S p-value are compared across schemes.

The application results confirm that the RSS scheme consistently produces
smaller K-S statistics and larger K-S p-values compared to the SRS scheme
across all sample sizes, demonstrating superior fit. The standard errors
of the RSS estimates are smaller than those of the SRS scheme, consistent
with the theoretical efficiency gains reported in the simulation study.
